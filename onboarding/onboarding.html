The one idea behind everything we sell

Getting introduced to a great man doesn't fix your dating life. If you can't attract, filter and keep the right man out in the world, an introduction to one of Nelly's clients ends the same way. He's a person with flaws and options, not a cure.

So we fix the woman's process first: how she presents, where she shows up, who she picks, and how she moves a man toward commitment. Nelly's line is that by the time she's done with you, you won't need her matchmaking database.

The whole job on one line

Help her see that something in her process is costing her the man she wants, get her to say she wants it fixed, and make joining easy. If she won't look inward at all, she isn't a fit. Thank her and move on.

03 · How she got to you

She applied to be matched. She doesn't know there's a program.

This shapes every call. She raised her hand for an introduction to a rich, serious man. Your job is to show her, kindly and honestly, that an introduction isn't what's missing.

1

Nelly posts a call to action

An Instagram story: comment or DM a keyword to be considered for her matchmaking database.

2

She fills out a long application

About 20 questions and 10 to 20 minutes of her time. That's real intent. The last question offers a free dating consultation, and most say yes and book.

3

Setter call (15 minutes)

Inbound consult or outbound dial. The setter runs the first quarter of the sale: what's going on, what she wants, what's in the way, whether she'll own it. She hears the program exists and that the investment runs $1K to $5K. Then the setter books the closing call.

4

Closing call (30 to 45 minutes)

Diagnose, pitch short, take payment on the call. Onboard her before you hang up.

5

Strategy call with Nelly, then the cohort

Nelly delivers. Sales hands off a clean, paid, onboarded client with the strategy call on the calendar.

What the calls taught us so far

The women who bought said their problem out loud and admitted they couldn't fix it alone. Once they did, the close was easy.

The women who didn't said some version of "I'm doing everything right, I just haven't met him." Then we guessed at what she was doing wrong, she disagreed, and the call ended with "let me think about it."

Long calls lose. The no's ran 47 to 76 minutes, mostly pitch. The fastest yes took 36 minutes, with a short pitch and payment taken on the call.

The price has to be said early. When the setter skipped the $1K to $5K range, the closer got "I had no idea it would cost that."

04 · Who you're talking to

She has built a good life. This is the one thing she can't figure out.

Who she is

  • Late 20s to mid 40s. Most are 30 to 36.
  • Has a career and earns her own money: sales director, consultant and investor, therapist, flight attendant, nurse, law student, business owner.
  • Busy. Works long hours, travels, tired by the evening.
  • Often faith-driven (Christian or Catholic) and family-oriented. Culture matters to many of them.
  • Follows Nelly and likes her tough love, at least on social.

What she wants

  • Marriage, and usually kids.
  • A man who is established and attractive and ready. She keeps finding two out of three.
  • A provider and a leader. Many want the option to slow down or stay home.
  • Emotional availability, real chemistry, shared values.
  • To stop wasting time. She'll say this word for word.

What's going wrong, in her words

"They say they want something serious, then nothing."Things fizzle at three to six months. She ends up asking "what are we?" and he's "happy where we're at."
"I attract the wrong men."Married men, younger men, men who want her to pay or to mother them, rich men who only want something casual.
"I barely go on dates."Three dates a year. One app, scrolled at night. Chats that never turn into a date. No friends to go out with.
"Am I too picky? Too much? Intimidating?"Men and friends have told her she asks for a lot. She half believes it.
"My friends settled."She won't. She'd rather be single than resent someone.

Four types you'll meet

Doing everything right

Successful, polished, busy social life, gets asked out. Can't get the men she wants to take her seriously. Hardest to close until she owns a leak.

Low volume

Apps only, few dates, shy or no one to go out with. Usually knows she isn't putting herself out there. Easier to close.

The giver

Caretaker. Cooks, cleans, carries the relationship, lets the wrong man in fast and stays too long.

Starting over

Recently divorced or out of a long relationship, often with a younger man. Knows what she doesn't want and doesn't want to lose more years.

05 · What she'll say

Common objections

Most objections are a symptom of skipped discovery. If she has named her problem and said she can't solve it alone, you'll hear very few. Full scripts are in the playbook. This is the short version.

Words that work

  • "Leak in the pipe." "One lever we haven't pulled." It makes the problem small and fixable, not her.
  • "You're too close to it to see it."
  • "Chooser, not chaser."
  • "There's nothing wrong with you. It's something you're doing, and that's good news, because you can change it."
  • Her own words, played back to her. "You said you're done wasting time."

Payment
