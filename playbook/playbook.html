Nelly Sudri  /  90-Day Dating Accelerator
A

The setter call

Your call is the first quarter of the sale. You are not selling the program and you are not her dating coach. You find out what's wrong, get her to admit part of it might be her, and book her with the closer while she still feels it.

Book her only when all five are true

  • She told you what's going wrong in her dating life.
  • She told you what she wants, and why now.
  • She named what's in the way, or agreed something might be.
  • She said she's willing to look inward and make a change.
  • She heard the investment range ($1K to $5K) and didn't walk.

Tone

Picture happy hour with a friend complaining about men. Empathy first, then honest tough love. You're curious and warm, and you don't let her off the hook.

Ask one question at a time. Then stop talking.

1. Open 0 to 1 min

Hi [name], this is [you] from Nelly's matchmaking team. Is now still a good time? … Looks like you filled out the application to be considered for introductions to Nelly's private male clients, and you booked a consultation about your dating life. Your consult was booked pretty far out. We've had a lot more applications than we expected since Nelly posted, so we're reaching out to see who we can help sooner. Do you have a few minutes now?

If she already had a call with us: "We got so many applications we're calling everyone back to make sure we actually helped. What ended up happening on that call?"

2. Discovery 1 to 8 min

Get her current situation, what she wants, and what's kept her from it. Use her answer to pick your next question.

First question I've got to ask: what's going on in your dating life right now? Give me the scoop.
How many dates have you been on in the last three months? Did any turn into second dates?
Do you feel like you're dating at a volume where you'd meet the right person by sheer math? Or is it more about quality?
What's the most frustrating part?
In a perfect world, who's the guy? What does he need to have?
What do you think has kept you from meeting him so far?

Play it back so she hears herself: "Correct me if I'm wrong. You're putting in effort, the guys say they want something serious up front, and then it turns out they don't?" Keep her out of a long story about her ex. You want patterns, not the whole history.

3. Ownership 8 to 11 min

This is the step that decides the sale. If she's blaming men, the city or luck, she isn't ready for a program yet. Don't diagnose her. That's Nelly's job. Your job is to get her open to the idea that something in her process is part of it.

Do you feel like this is luck and it just hasn't happened yet? Or is it possible there's something you're overlooking, in how you meet them, turn that into dates, or turn dates into something real?
Would you be shocked if Nelly looked at your Hinge conversations, your texts, how you are on dates, and found the one thing that's making this not work? And if you fixed it, everything changed?
Let's assume your standards are reasonable. If you had to take full ownership and look completely inward, where might you be missing the mark?

If she's still blaming men, weigh in:

Can I weigh in for a second? There are men out there who make money, look good and are serious. You agree? So something has kept you from them. And if it were just about getting introductions, you'd have found him by now. Think about how many men you've met in your life. Is there even 10% of you that thinks there might be something you're doing, or not doing, that attracts the wrong ones?

Once she says yes, lock it in: "Are you willing to look into that and make a change so you actually get what you want?" Then: "Do you feel like you could use a hand from Nelly with that?"

4. Next step and the investment 11 to 13 min

Give two options, and recommend the second. Don't make the free option sound equal, and don't describe the program. The second option is a longer consultation, not the coaching itself.

So there are two ways we can go. One: you stay in the database. If a client of Nelly's fits, we reach out. I'll be honest with you, though. Nelly works with a handful of male clients at a time and thousands of women applied, so the math isn't great, and nothing there is guaranteed. Even if we do introduce you, who's to say the same thing doesn't happen with him?

Two, and this is what I'd do in your shoes: I set you up for a longer consultation to dig into what's actually been in the way and what the fix is. If it makes sense to work together after that, you'll get options. If not, no pressure.
Being upfront: if you do end up working together, it's not free. It typically runs between $1K and $5K depending on your situation and what you need. Does that still keep us in the game?

"What's the catch?" or "Are you selling me something?": "There's nothing I'm selling you today. The only thing on the table is the longer consultation. About half the people who book aren't really serious, and that's fine. We're prioritizing the half who are. If that's you, let's get you in sooner."

5. Book it 13 to 15 min

  • Ask for the booking. Don't wait for her to offer. "Let's get you in. What does tomorrow look like?"
  • Book the soonest slot she can make, within 48 hours if possible. Every extra day lowers the show rate.
  • Ask her time zone before you read times. Read times in her zone.
  • Tell her to cancel her old far-out booking. Stay on the line until she confirms the invite arrived.
  • Set the call up so she shows prepared: "Block 45 minutes and be somewhere you can talk. If you have recent texts or your profiles handy, even better."

If she won't look inward at all

Totally fair. If you really feel you're doing most things right and it's a matter of time, keep doing what you're doing. I'll keep you in the database. If you have a rough few weeks and start thinking there might be something you can tweak, reach back out and we'll get you in.

Setter mistakes we've already made

  • Diagnosing her yourself ("girl, I think that's your answer"). You lose authority and she argues with you.
  • Pitching the program, or inventing features: "one-on-one with Nelly," "men outside the database," "she'll tailor the group for you." Say less.
  • Asking "which option sounds more like you? There's no wrong answer." She picks free every time.
  • Telling her to wait, or tying the booking to whether she can pay right now.
  • Skipping the price range. The closer then hears "I had no idea it cost that."
  • Getting her to admit something real ("I drink on dates," "I let them in too fast") and then not using it.
  • Saying you're new on the team, or guessing at how the database works. If you don't know, say Nelly's team will cover it.
  • Fumbling the calendar. Ask the time zone first, then book.

B

The closing call

Spend most of the call on diagnosis. Keep the pitch short. Take payment before you hang up. If the discovery is right, you'll barely need objection handling.

1. Frame 0 to 2 min

Thanks for making the time. Before we jump in, I want to understand where you're at in your dating life, what's been hard, and what you're hoping for. If I think we can help, I'll walk you through what working with Nelly looks like. If I don't, I'll tell you. Sound good? So what made you book this call?

If the setter told you something, use it: "[Setter] gave me a little briefing on your call."

2. Discovery 2 to 18 min

Ask, listen, then ask again based on what she said. You're a consultant trying to find the real constraint. Don't decide what her problem is before she gets there.

Where she is

Have you been single for a while or is it recent? What happened with the last one?
How are you meeting men right now? Apps, in person, through friends? How many dates in the last few months?

What she wants (high level only)

What's on the list for him? What's a true non-negotiable and what's a nice-to-have?

Get height, income range, kids, faith, one or two intangibles. Don't go 20 items deep and don't hand out advice. Then summarize it back.

Do you believe a man like that exists?

What's in the way

You're attractive, you've built a real life, you know what you want, and you say he exists. So why do you think you haven't ended up in something serious with him yet?

Drill every vague answer:

  • "I'm not meeting them." "You mean they're not showing up at your door, or something else?"
  • "They're intimidated by me." "What do you mean by that? What happens?"
  • "It just hasn't happened." "Tell me more. Is it where you're meeting them, something on the dates, or how things go after?"
  • "I've done everything." "Have you done anything differently than what you're doing now?"

Why now

On a scale of 0 to 10, how much of a priority is this right now? … Why that number?
Say nothing changes and a year from now you're in the same spot. What's going through your mind?
And a year from now, where do you want to be, realistically?

Ask this on every call

What would this have to look like for it to be a no-brainer for you? First, what's a realistic outcome at the end of 90 days? And second, what would need to be part of it for you to feel confident it gets you there?

If she says "find my husband in 90 days," be honest: "I love that you're motivated, but I'm not going to promise something no one can deliver. What's an outcome you think is realistic?" Her answer becomes your pitch.

3. Diagnosis 18 to 24 min

Discovery ends one of two ways.

She names her own problem

"I only go on three dates a year." "I let the wrong guys in too fast." Good. Ask why she hasn't fixed it, and find the gap.

If you know that's it, what's stopped you from changing it?

Usually it's that she doesn't know how. "Would it help to learn exactly how to filter and attract the right ones?" She just made the argument for the program.

She's "doing everything right"

Don't guess at her problem. A guess she disagrees with costs you the sale. Weigh in and get her to look inward.

Can I tell you what I'm thinking? 99% of the time it's one clear leak she's too close to see.

Then the gate question below.

There's something in your dating process. It could be how you present, how you attract, how you filter, even the message you send between dates. It can be the smallest thing, like how you look at him on the first date or one text that tells him what category to put you in. And if everything were perfect, let's be honest, you'd probably have your partner by now. I'm not talking about a drastic transformation or changing who you are. If there's low-hanging fruit, why not fix it?
If you looked at everything, from the moment you lock eyes with a guy to the moment he tells you he's not looking for anything serious, you don't think you'd find a single thing? … Can you pinpoint it now, or would it take a deeper look?
That's what the strategy call is for. All I need to know is this: are you willing to look inward and make the changes, without compromising who you are, just stepping into the best version of yourself?

A yes here is the close. A hard no means she isn't a fit, so thank her and end the call. If you do have specific evidence from what she said, use it. Nelly closed Michelle's call by pointing out that the guy who "wasn't pursuing enough" took her to a game hoping to sleep with her, so the issue wasn't exposure. It was getting the men she wants to take her seriously.

4. Pitch 24 to 30 min, five minutes max

Only what solves her problem. The rest she can learn on Skool.

Here's how this works. It's a 90-day program. First, you get a one-on-one strategy call with Nelly, within days. She goes through everything end to end: your profiles, your texts, your recent dates, where you're meeting men. You'll leave with an attack plan for the first few weeks. Then every week there's a live call with Nelly and a small group of other marriage-minded women, where you report what's happening and adjust in real time. In between, you can message Nelly directly, like "he sent this, what do I say?" or "what do I wear Friday?" And there's a library of training you won't find on her social. Based on what you told me, I'd expect her to focus on [her leak 1] and [her leak 2].

Does that make sense? What questions do you have?

Answer questions in one or two sentences. Long answers make her second-guess herself. Be honest about results: women in the program get into relationships, and some have gotten engaged and married, but not usually inside 90 days. "If all this does is get you a lot closer, is it worth it?"

5. Price and ask 30 to 33 min

When Nelly did this kind of work through bigger matchmaking companies, clients invested $10K, $20K, sometimes more. She's streamlined it into a small group so it's more accessible. The program is $5,000. If you join on this call, it's $3,000 total. That's the on-call price, and it's real. If you come back later, it's $5,000.

Based on everything we talked about, how are you feeling about moving forward?

Then stop talking. If she says yes, go straight to payment. If she raises an objection, go to Part C and ask questions instead of explaining.

6. Pay and onboard before you hang up 33 to 40 min

  • Confirm her email. Send the payment link in the chat and by text.
  • Payment: $3,000 in full, or split so the total is still $3,000. Use the split only if she asks.
  • Stay on until it clears. If a card declines, have her try another card or use the backup payment method.
  • Send the Skool invite (Queen of Hearts) while she's on, and have her join.
  • Book her strategy call with Nelly for the soonest slot she can do.
  • Ask her to send recent texts, date examples and photos before the strategy call so Nelly can audit.
  • Tell her what happens next and when the first group call is. End on her goal: "I can't wait to see where you are in 90 days."
  • Log it in GHL and post the close in Slack.
C

Objection handling

Ask, don't tell. Every objection gets a question first. Let her talk, find what it's really about, then answer only that. Most "think about it" calls are doubt or money dressed up politely.

"I need to think about it." / "Can I sleep on it?"

Absolutely. What specifically are you going to think through?
What exactly would make you sway one way or the other?
I'm happy to give you time. What I'm unclear on is what will be different tomorrow, without me there to answer questions, compared to right now with me here?
And if questions come up later, in what case would my answer actually change your decision?

If she still wants the night, commit her to making the decision, not to joining:

Here's what I'd suggest. If we hang up now, life gets in the way. Your friend isn't free to talk, you sleep badly, and a week goes by. So let's do a refundable $500 deposit. It holds the $3,000 price and tells both of us you're taking the decision seriously. We'll get on a quick call tomorrow, and it's a yes or a no. If it's a no, you get all $500 back. If it's a yes, it goes toward the program.

Book the follow-up call before you hang up. A hold without a deposit is a polite no.

"It's a lot of money."

Totally fair. Can I ask, is it the money itself, or is it not being sure it'll make a difference for you?

Let her talk. If it's doubt: "What would make you feel confident it would make a difference?" Then go back to her own words from discovery. If it's really cash flow, offer the split so the total is still $3,000. Don't drop the price.

Besides the money, is there anything else making you hesitate? … So if the money were solved, you'd want to do this?

"I don't have time." / "My schedule is crazy."

It's not meant to feel like a second job. It's one call a week, recorded if you miss it, plus chat when you need it. You layer it into the life you already have.
If time weren't an issue, would you do this today? … So what's the real hesitation?

The women who end up married don't treat their career as the reason they can't. They make time for what they want.

"I bought a program before and never used it."

What got in the way last time?
What would need to be different for you to actually show up this time?

Then match her answer to the structure. She gets a one-on-one strategy call within days, live weekly check-ins with a group of women moving in the same direction, Nelly in her pocket on chat, and the Skool app on her phone. The program is built so she isn't alone with a login.

"I'm going to wait until I move / after the holidays."

What do you think will be different there? … You told me [you had dates every week in New York and still didn't land it]. So is it really the location?
If you start now, you arrive with a strategy instead of starting from zero. Waiting means November, December and the holidays go by on the same pattern you told me you're done with.

"Can I just be in the database?" / "Can Nelly match me?"

You're already in it, and it's free. Nelly only takes male clients for matchmaking. But think about it: what makes you think one more introduction ends differently than the men you've already met? If you can't attract, filter and keep the right man out in the world, an introduction from Nelly ends the same way. The goal of the program is that you won't need her database. And the women in it get priority for introductions, because Nelly can vouch for them.

"Is there a guarantee?" / "What if I do all this and I'm still single?"

The only guarantees in life are death and taxes. If I told you you'd find your husband in 90 days, I'd be lying. What I can tell you is that in 90 days you can build your confidence, fix how you present, get in the right rooms, learn to filter, and have real options. If all this does is get you a lot closer, is that worth it?

Never improvise a guarantee or a "free until you get results" promise. We don't offer one.

"I've never paid for anything like this."

Fair. What have you tried so far, and how's it worked? … So if you keep doing what you've been doing, where do you realistically end up?

"I need to ask my friend / family."

Of course. What do you think they'll say? … And if they said go for it, would you do it today?

If yes, the decision is hers and it's about reassurance, so go back to her goal. If you need to, offer the deposit and a follow-up call.

"How long has Nelly done this?" / "How many women has she helped?"

Years. First with other companies, then on her own, then as the face of Blush. She's worked with thousands of successful men as a matchmaker, which is why she knows exactly what they want. She has run this program before, and women from it have gotten into relationships, gotten engaged and gotten married.

When it goes sideways

If she won't look inward at all, don't force it. Thank her, keep her in the database and end on good terms. If she asks for something not on the deliverables list, don't promise it. Tell her you'll check, and ask Mitch.
